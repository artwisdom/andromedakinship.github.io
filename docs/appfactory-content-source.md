## Storm artwork

Following the owner's visual feedback, the planet has a regional storm front over roughly two fifths of its surface. Layered billows, fine cloud detail, and modest local curling replace the all-over striped whirlpools. Calmer surface remains outside the front. The planetary rings, app catalog, and existing logo are preserved.

The September 9 refinement replaces the coarse procedural appearance with an original generated cloud texture, a raised deck and drifting veil, filtered sampling, and a darker charcoal/blue-violet palette. Quiet surface regions now read as a dark crust. Rings remain but are more subdued. The reveal starts at 70% instead of 76%, with a slightly earlier approach and a longer galactic background overlap. Texture loading, fallback, cleanup, render resolution, and cache-version checks were added. See `storm-texture-source.md` for asset provenance and the exact prompt.

Lightning is now diffuse under-cloud illumination at orbital scale, with no visible bolt paths. One seeded event at a time changes location, onset, and strength. Each 1.65-second scheduling slot contains one gradual rise/fall and a dark gap, preventing simultaneous globe-wide discharges. The shader limits illumination to the weather region. No whole-screen exposure modulation or extra render pass was added. Pause/reduced-motion/hidden-tab behavior freezes the existing animation clock. Tests cover event continuity, range, spacing, distribution, weather coverage, and pause/resize/resume behavior. This is not formal photosensitivity certification.

The planet is now 1,000 times smaller relative to the original galaxy, with a late logarithmic dive before its reveal. A separate local render frame avoids losing surface precision. The initial visible world occupies less than 3% of viewport height in the tested reveal interval; the final desktop/phone viewing distances remain unchanged. The camera locks onto the destination before it becomes visible, so it cannot overshoot its target at the new scale.

The localhost-only `?qa=scene-debug` fixture exposes graphics errors, flight position, and the active cloud event on the canvas. Its freeze/sample buttons hold a single normal event for inspection; flight buttons scroll to representative journey positions. No test control is emitted into public HTML or the production bundle.
