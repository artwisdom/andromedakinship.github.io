How to monitor FDA recalls
in real time

For a medical-device, pharma, or food company, an FDA recall isn't just news — it can trigger a distribution decision, a customer notification, or an audit question within hours. Here's how to catch recalls as they publish instead of finding out from a weekly digest or a customer email.

Where the FDA actually publishes

FDA recall information lives in a few public places, and the trick is using the fast one and the detailed one together:

  • FDA recalls RSS feed — the official "Recalls, Market Withdrawals & Safety Alerts" feed. This is your fastest trigger: it carries the headline the moment an action posts.
  • openFDA enforcement APIs — structured records for device, drug, and food recalls, including recall classification, recalling firm, reason, product description, and distribution scope. This is the detail that fills in around the headline.
  • openFDA 510(k) and adverse-event (MAUDE) endpoints — for device teams tracking new clearances and adverse-event reports alongside recalls.

The headline often publishes before the full structured record is available, so the most useful setup pairs the RSS trigger with the openFDA record and updates the alert as classification and distribution detail catch up.

Read the recall class first

Every FDA recall carries a classification that tells you how serious it is — it's the single most useful triage signal:

  • Class I — a reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death. Drop everything.
  • Class II — may cause temporary or medically reversible harm; serious harm is unlikely.
  • Class III — unlikely to cause harm, but still a violation worth noting.

If you only have time to act on one thing, act on Class I. Building your alerting so the class is visible at a glance saves your team from reading every notice in full just to triage.

Why real-time beats the weekly digest

A weekly newsletter or a manual morning check of FDA.gov leaves a gap measured in days. For time-sensitive recall and adverse-event signals, that gap is where the risk lives — a shipment goes out, a customer calls, an auditor asks. Real-time monitoring shrinks the gap to roughly the length of one polling cycle.

FAQ

Where does the FDA publish recalls?
On its Recalls, Market Withdrawals & Safety Alerts page (with an RSS feed), with structured data available through the openFDA enforcement APIs for drug, device, and food recalls.
What do FDA recall classes mean?
Class I is the most serious (reasonable probability of serious harm or death), Class II involves temporary or reversible harm, and Class III is unlikely to cause harm. The FDA assigns the class.
How do I get notified in real time?
