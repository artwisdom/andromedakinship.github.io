How to track SEC & FINRA actions
in real time

Financial compliance teams answer to a patchwork of regulators, and the penalty-bearing actions move fast. The challenge isn't understanding any one rule — it's covering all the agencies at once without someone opening seven websites every morning.

The seven regulators worth watching

Exactly which apply depends on your charter and business model, but most US fintech and banking compliance teams watch some combination of:

  • SEC — press releases covering enforcement, rulemaking, and guidance.
  • FINRA — regulatory notices for broker-dealers.
  • OCC — news releases and supervisory bulletins for national banks.
  • FDIC — press releases, including monthly enforcement roundups.
  • NYDFS — New York enforcement actions (a bellwether for state regulators).
  • FinCEN — AML, BSA, and sanctions-adjacent notices.
  • FTC — consumer-protection actions touching payments, lending, BNPL, crypto, debanking, and data brokers.

A state-chartered lender and a registered broker-dealer don't care about the same subset — which is exactly why filtering matters as much as coverage.

Triage by severity, not by agency

When you're watching seven sources, volume is the enemy. The fix is to sort by urgency rather than reading everything:

  • Urgent — enforcement actions carrying a penalty, or rules effective immediately. These can't wait.
  • Notice — proposed rulemaking and guidance. Read soon; comment if it affects you.
  • Info — routine announcements for background awareness.

If every alert carries a severity label, your team can act on the penalty-bearing items immediately and batch the rest.

FAQ

Which financial regulators should we monitor?
It depends on your charter, but the common set is the SEC, FINRA, OCC, FDIC, state regulators like NYDFS, FinCEN, and the FTC for consumer-protection matters touching payments, lending, and crypto.
